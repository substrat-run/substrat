# K-55 — A platform host's standing principal holds a system-only key through a role declared `holder: 'system'`

> Proposed 2026-10-18, awaiting ratification. It records the mechanism the dashboard's assistant host (P1 of [#2283](https://github.com/substrat-run/substrat/issues/2283)) needs, and the only one of its kind.
>
> **Since #2430 the reservation is also refused at push and at a host's doors; see "Evidence and limits".**
>
> **The reserved-key table this names is now a declaration; the decision stands.** A key is reserved to a system principal by `holder: 'system'` on its module's manifest permission entry (engine-assistant declares it on `assistant:run`), and `tools/reserved-keys.mts` reads the reserved keys off the loaded manifests rather than a table of its own. A system-held role may still carry only such keys, and no person's role or entity grant shape may carry one.

**A role may declare `holder: 'system'`.** `roleDefinition` gains an optional `holder`, `person` (absent means this) or `system`. A system-held role is the one role shape that may carry a key reserved to a system principal (`tools/reserved-keys.mts`, today `assistant:run`). It may carry nothing else, so a platform host's authority is exactly the reserved keys it is declared with. `lint:permissions` refuses a reserved key in a `person` role or an entity grant shape, as before, and refuses a system-held role holding a key that is not reserved. The checked-in `PERMISSIONS.md` renders system-held roles in their own section (§2a) and marks them in the coverage table, so granting or widening one is a line in a reviewed diff.

**Why this, and not a role defined outside `ROLES`.** The host that runs a thread must call `assistant/acquire-run` and `assistant/append-events` in the middle of a request. The sanctioned system holders, a schedule's or a peer's `permissions`, are not callable that way. A principal holding a role defined directly with `host.admin.defineRole`, outside the declared surface, would work, and would hold a powerful key where no artifact shows it. The marker puts the same principal in the artifact.

**What it is not.** It is not the long-term answer. The RFC's §6.0 ends with the assistant acting for the user under a stamped actor and a ceiling (#1824 part 2, a generalised K-42), where no standing principal holds the key. This record is the stopgap that makes the first host reviewable, and the marker is what lets a later change remove the principal cleanly.

## Evidence and limits

- **Held by** `tools/reserved-keys.test.mts`: the function, and the tool on fixture verticals (a system role passes and renders; a system role with `assistant:use` exits 2; `holder: 'person'` is the same as none).
- **Enforced at runtime by reserved keys and the stored role holder (#2430, #2444).** A host reads the reserved keys off the modules it registers and the modules its declared `permissions` lists, and refuses them at its doors:
  - The person-facing role doors (an invite's scope role, a role change, a membership add) refuse any role carrying a reserved key, and `canAssign` answers the same, for taking such a role away as for giving it.
  - A raw grant of one to a principal or an org is refused, `ctx.grant` included, whoever the caller is.
  - So is a grant of one to a connection, which acts for the external provider holding its token, and `lint:connector-grants` refuses a connector whose standing grants name one. A connection grant already recorded is skipped and reported when a provision or reconcile delivers it, never failing the provision.
  - A reconcile of declared entity-grant shapes refuses a shape holding one, before it tops up any holder or records what a binding link grants.
  - The platform's role assignment refuses a role that mixes reserved keys with others. Since #2444, a newly defined role made only of reserved keys is admitted only when its stored holder is `system`; a pre-migration role with no stored holder keeps the earlier key-only decision until it is redefined.
- **Roles a host defines and seats itself (#2447).** A role definition (`defineRole`, and the role set a CP-less provision or restore projects) is judged before it is written: a person's role holding a reserved key is refused, and so is a system-held role holding a key a person may hold. A redefinition is judged against the role it replaces and its seats: a key added to a role is judged as the new definition; a stored system-held role redefined as a person's is judged with its stored keys counted as reserved; a stored person's role cannot become `holder: 'system'` while anyone but a system principal holds a seat in it, since its seats were made through a person's door (a role stored before the holder was recorded and made only of reserved keys may, #2480); a first definition is judged as before. So a role cannot be widened after it was assigned, by a key or by a holder change. The provisioning owner seat and the owner hand-over are person doors. Every person door refuses a role stored `holder: 'system'` from the holder alone. A host that registers no modules counts the keys of the tenant's system-held roles as reserved when a role is defined. A refusal writes nothing, so nothing is revoked: a seat holding a key reserved after it was seated keeps working, and a directory fan-out of a stored definition is not judged again. `lint:role-seat-paths` refuses a host path that seats or defines a role without asking.
- **A release that makes a seated person's role system-held passes push and is refused at reconcile (#2447).** Push and promote cannot see seats: the promote diff marks the moved holder (`roleChanges[].holder`) and refuses nothing. The scope's reconcile (`provisionScopeLocal`) then refuses the role set, naming the tenant, the scope and the role, at every scope where anyone but a system principal holds a seat in it (an archived scope's seats count), on every pass. It writes nothing, so nothing is half-applied, but it holds that scope's **whole** reconcile: the release's other roles, the `system:` grants of its new schedules, delivered connection grants and entity-grant retirements are not applied there while the scope serves the new code. The remedy is per scope: take the persons' seats in that role back, or ship a release that keeps the role person-held. Refusing the holder change at push or promote, where `roleChanges[].holder` is already computed, belongs with #2446. The same refusal meets a **system-held role a release drops and a later release adds back** while its K-55 host principal is still seated: a CP-less projection removes the dropped row and leaves the seat, and what kind of principal holds a seat is not recorded, so the seat cannot be told from a person's. That scope's reconcile is held the same way; the remedy per scope is to take the host principal's seat back and give it again after the role is back, or to keep the role defined across releases. Keeping a dropped row as a tombstone that records its holder would let the rule tell the two apart; that is a follow-up.
- **A key a host knows nothing about is admitted (fail-open, deliberate).** A host judges only the reservations of the modules it registers or lists and of the tenant's system-held roles. Refusing every key it cannot place would refuse every role a module-less host is given; the push is where the whole surface is judged. A key reserved only through a schedule's `permissions`, which no system-held role holds, is not caught on a host that does not register its module (#2446).
- **Not checked yet.** On Cloudflare, the scope-side half of a role judgement is read outside the unit that writes: the coordinator reads a scope's seats before the ControlPlaneDO's `defineRole`, and a local provision reads the scope's roles and seats in one call and projects in the next, so a seat made in that window is not seen. A role defined on a Cloudflare coordinator for a tenant with no scope yet is judged by the coordinator's modules alone (the scope class's reservations are read from one of the tenant's scopes), and the first scope's projection of it is not judged again. Grants a host seats or composes itself (an install's owner grants, on a host that does not register the vertical's modules), the directory record a shared control plane keeps of a connection grant whose serving deployment refuses it (it confers nothing, but stays listed until revoked), and grants and shape bindings recorded before these checks existed or before the key was reserved, with what reaches a person through them later, an older build's re-seat after a rollback included.
- **What that door cannot tell.** A stored role now records its holder (#2444), but the door does not know whether the assignee is a person or a host principal. A correctly marked system-held role can still be assigned to a person at that door. One principal given two roles there can hold reserved keys and person keys together, which the engine test kit does, deliberately, to build a test principal. Telling a person from the host principal it is meant for needs a record of what kind of principal each one is, which nothing keeps yet.
- **Not decided here:** the host principal's lifecycle per team, and the actor and ceiling model that replaces it.

## Why

**The key is reserved because it writes the assistant's side of the record.** A person holding `assistant:run` could append events to their own thread and have the record say the assistant wrote them. The record is only evidence if that key is out of every person's reach.

**An exception that is declared is reviewable; one that is merely made is not.** The repo's checkpoint is a human reading a diff. A grant that never appears in the diff is outside it.
