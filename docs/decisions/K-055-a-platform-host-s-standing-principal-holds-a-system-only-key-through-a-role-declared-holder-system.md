# K-55 — A platform host's standing principal holds a system-only key through a role declared `holder: 'system'`

> Proposed 2026-10-18, awaiting ratification. It records the mechanism the dashboard's assistant host (P1 of [#2283](https://github.com/substrat-run/substrat/issues/2283)) needs, and the only one of its kind.
>
> **Since #2430 the reservation is also refused at push and at a host's doors; see "Evidence and limits".**
>
> **The reserved-key table this names is now a declaration; the decision stands.** A key is reserved to a system principal by `holder: 'system'` on its module's manifest permission entry (engine-assistant declares it on `assistant:run`), and `tools/reserved-keys.mts` reads the reserved keys off the loaded manifests rather than a table of its own. A system-held role may still carry only such keys, and no person's role or entity grant shape may carry one.

**A role may declare `holder: 'system'`.** `roleDefinition` gains an optional `holder`, `person` (absent means this) or `system`. A system-held role is the one role shape that may carry a key reserved to a system principal (`tools/reserved-keys.mts`, today `assistant:run`). It may carry nothing else, so a platform host's authority is exactly the reserved keys it is declared with. `lint:permissions` refuses a reserved key in a `person` role or an entity grant shape, as before, and refuses a system-held role holding a key that is not reserved. The checked-in `PERMISSIONS.md` renders system-held roles in their own section (§2a) and marks them in the coverage table, so granting or widening one is a line in a reviewed diff.

**Why this, and not a role defined outside `ROLES`.** The host that runs a thread must call `assistant/acquire-run` and `assistant/append-events` in the middle of a request. The sanctioned system holders, a schedule's or a peer's `permissions`, are not callable that way. A principal holding a role defined directly with `host.admin.defineRole`, outside the declared surface, would work, and would hold a powerful key where no artifact shows it. The marker puts the same principal in the artifact.

**What it is not.** It is not the long-term answer. The RFC's §6.0 ends with the assistant acting for the user under a stamped actor and a ceiling (#1824 part 2, a generalised K-42), where no standing principal holds the key. This record is the stopgap that makes the first host reviewable, and the marker is what lets a later change remove the principal cleanly.

## Evidence and limits

- **Held by** `tools/reserved-keys.test.mts`: the function, and the tool on fixture verticals (a system role passes and renders; a system role with `assistant:use` exits 2; `holder: 'person'` is the same as none).
- **Enforced at runtime by reserved keys and the stored role holder (#2430, #2444).** A host reads the reserved keys off the modules it registers and the modules its declared `permissions` lists, and refuses them at its doors:
  - The person-facing role doors (an invite's scope role, a role change, a membership add) refuse any role carrying a reserved key, and `canAssign` answers the same, for taking such a role away as for giving it.
  - A raw grant of one to a principal or an org is refused, `ctx.grant` included, whoever the caller is.
  - So is a grant of one to a connection, which acts for the external provider holding its token, and `lint:connector-grants` refuses a connector whose standing grants name one. A connection grant already recorded is skipped and reported when a provision or reconcile delivers it, never failing the provision.
  - A reconcile of declared entity-grant shapes refuses a shape holding one, before it tops up any holder or records what a binding link grants.
  - The platform's role assignment refuses a role that mixes reserved keys with others. Since #2444, a newly defined role made only of reserved keys is admitted only when its stored holder is `system`; a pre-migration role with no stored holder keeps the earlier key-only decision until it is redefined.
- **Roles a host defines and seats itself (#2447).** A role definition (`defineRole`, and the role set a CP-less provision or restore projects) is judged before it is written: a person's role holding a reserved key is refused, and so is a system-held role holding a key a person may hold. A redefinition is judged against the role it replaces and its seats: a key added to a role is judged as the new definition; a stored system-held role redefined as a person's is judged with its stored keys counted as reserved; a `holder: 'system'` definition of a key anyone but a system principal holds a seat in is refused unless a system-held definition is stored for it — whether the stored row is a person's, a pre-#2444 row with no holder, or absent (a seat can outlive its row, and what kind of principal holds a seat is not recorded); scope-level seats in every scope that is not reaped count, one archived straight from provisioning included (it can hold a seat); such a scope is read without creating storage it never had, and on Cloudflare, where a DO cannot be asked without waking it, one whose directory row records no migration fails the definition closed where it could decide it (its seats, or the class's reservations when no other scope gives them). The row's migration count is not a mark of instantiation: a scope of a class with no module migrations records none and can hold a seat. While such a scope is the tenant's only one, an identical re-apply of a person's role and any new role are refused until it is unarchived or reaped (both work; a reap needs no bound hostname); no product path reaches this today, the dashboard, the one Cloudflare caller of `defineRole`, running a class with migrations, and tenant-level seats are read from the directory, never from a scope's projected copy. A first definition of a key nobody holds is judged by the key rules alone. So a role cannot be widened after it was assigned, by a key or by a holder change. The provisioning owner seat and the owner hand-over are person doors. Every person door refuses a role stored `holder: 'system'` from the holder alone. A host that registers no modules counts the keys of the tenant's system-held roles as reserved when a role is defined. A refusal writes nothing, so nothing is revoked: a seat holding a key reserved after it was seated keeps working, and a directory fan-out of a stored definition is not judged again. `lint:role-seat-paths` refuses a host path that seats or defines a role without asking.
- **A release that makes a seated person's role system-held passes push, and its promote is refused (#2447, #2446).** Push cannot see seats; the promote diff marks the moved holder (`roleChanges[].holder`). A prod promote of a pushed vertical whose release declares a system-held role asks every scope it moves onto the release (each live primary scope on the serving script, and a private vertical's owned installs that the adopt moves) for the roles it stores and the role keys seated there, and judges the release's role set with the reconcile's own rule and the release's registry marks. A scope that would refuse it refuses the promote: 409, nothing served, adopted or moved. A release with no system-held role asks no scope; an embedded vertical, whose roles no reconcile of the registry defines, is asked nothing; one promote reads at most a capped number of scopes, and more is a 503 naming the two ways through: raise the cap, or a staff-only acknowledgement (`roleStateUnjudged`) that skips the read and is recorded in the promote's audit row. A deployment that predates the read (no route, a 404, or no host method, its 501) is moved unjudged and counted in the answer, and any other failed read refuses the promote. A confined caller sees only its own scopes in these answers, other tenants as counts. Where the read was skipped or a seat was made after it, the scope's reconcile (`provisionScopeLocal`) then refuses the role set, naming the tenant, the scope and the role, at every scope where anyone but a system principal holds a seat in it (an archived scope's seats count), on every pass. It writes nothing, so nothing is half-applied, but it holds that scope's **whole** reconcile: the release's other roles, the `system:` grants of its new schedules, delivered connection grants and entity-grant retirements are not applied there while the scope serves the new code. The remedy is per scope: take the persons' seats in that role back, or ship a release that keeps the role person-held. **A role a release drops is kept as a tombstone (#2531).** A CP-less projection no longer deletes the row of a role the new set leaves out: it marks it revoked, with the keys and the holder it had, and the seats stay. A tombstone grants nothing (every reader that expands a role skips a revoked row, so a seat in a dropped role authorizes as it did when the row was deleted) and it is kept for good, as evidence (K-21); adding the role back writes the row live again. The rule reads a **system-held** tombstone as the stored system-held definition it was, in both branches: a later release that adds the key back as system-held is not refused for the K-55 host principal still seated in it, and one that adds it back as a person's role counts the tombstone's keys as reserved. That is parity with keeping the role defined across releases, **not proof**: the platform's door can seat a person in a system-held role whether its row is live or dropped (above). A tombstone of a person's role, or of a row with no holder, is no evidence of anything a system principal holds, so a release that adds that key back as system-held while it is seated is refused as before. A scope that dropped a row before tombstones were kept has none, and its reconcile is held the same way as before; the remedy there is to take the host principal's seat back and give it again after the role is back. The promote's read carries the tombstones (`RoleState.dropped`); a deployment that predates them sends none and a control plane that predates them ignores them, and either way the promote judges as strictly as before. **A K-55 role stored before holders were recorded (#2480), with its host principal seated, can never be upgraded in place:** no door seats a `system:` subject, so its seat counts, and declaring it `holder: 'system'` is refused. On a CP-less reconcile it is refused on every pass (that scope's whole reconcile is held while the scope keeps serving); the remedy is to take the host principal's seat back, reconcile, and seat it again. At a restore the pre-check answers 409 and nothing is restored; a seat inside a dump cannot be taken back first, so the only remedy in the product is a new role key. The population is roles stored on adapter 0.154 or 0.155 that never reconciled under 0.156 to 0.159, or dumps taken before 2026-10-21; no vertical in this repository uses a system-held role.
- **A staff restore or import on a host the control plane runs (#2555).** `restoreScope` and `importScope` load a dump's scope-level seats under the tenant's roles as the directory stores them now. Before anything is replaced (before the orphan-sweep fence, and for an import before the scope is created), that role set is judged as a restore's projection is, against the dump's own role rows and seats (`dumpRoleRefusal`, the one helper both adapters call). Only the roles the dump seats or stores are asked about, so a directory role the dump has no part in never refuses a load; the directory's own doors judge it. A dump holding a seat in a role the directory has since made system-held is refused, unless the dump stores that role system-held itself. A dump with no role rows (a pure-SQLite scope file, or a Cloudflare scope that reads its roles over RPC) says nothing about the definition its seats were made under, so such a seat is judged as a person's. That fails closed on purpose: such a dump holding a seat in a role the directory stores system-held is refused even when the seat was given through the platform's door, and the shared suite pins the two readings side by side. **The consequence:** on a host whose dump carries no role rows, a scope holding a seat in a system-held role cannot be restored from its own export, whether for a backup, an escrow or a point-in-time restore, since a seat inside a dump cannot be taken back first. No role on a host the control plane runs is system-held today, so no scope is in that state yet. The remedy is an export that carries the directory's roles for the tenant, so the dump can show which definition its seats were made under; that is a follow-up, and needed before the first system-held role is seated on such a host. The refusal is one of two fixed sentences naming the role key only, and nothing is loaded; the second says that the dump carries no definition of the role, where that is the reason. The verb throws `forbidden`; the control plane's restore route answers it as it answers any dump it cannot load, 422 with the sentence in `detail`. On the pure host an import whose second judgement refuses has already created its scope, and takes it down again before answering, so a retry meets no scope. That teardown is best effort: the refusal is the answer whatever it does, a teardown that fails files a staff-only ops-failure row (no tenant on it) and leaves the scope in `provisioning` for staff to reap, and no admin row is written for it. A staff restore or import takes a dump the staff caller supplies, and that dump is trusted input: its provenance is the staff caller's, as the route that accepts it is staff-only; what is judged is how the tenant's roles apply to it now. A seat forged into a role that the dump and the directory both store system-held is not caught, since what kind of principal holds a seat is not recorded. A dump's tombstones count as a scope's do (#2531): the judgement reads a system-held one as the stored system-held definition it was, so a seat in a system-held role the dump keeps only as a tombstone passes as it would were the row live, and a role the dump keeps only as a tombstone is one it defines. A restore into a CP-less deployment was already judged (#2447). The pure host judges again inside the load's transaction, against the roles stored at that moment. On Cloudflare the directory is read before the scope's load, so a role defined between the two is not judged against the dump (below). Bringing the scope's own copy of the tenant's permission state in line with the directory as the load's last step is #2555's second step.
- **A key a host knows nothing about is admitted (fail-open, deliberate).** A host judges only the reservations of the modules it registers or lists and of the tenant's system-held roles. Refusing every key it cannot place would refuse every role a module-less host is given; the push is where the whole surface is judged. A key reserved only through a schedule's `permissions`, which no system-held role holds, is not caught on a host that does not register its module (#2446).
- **A CP-less role set is judged inside the unit that writes it (#2531).** On Cloudflare, a local provision or reconcile (`provisionScopeLocal`) and a restore's role repair (`projectRolesLocal`) judge their role set a second time inside the projection's own transaction (`applyJudgedProjectionReply`), against the roles, tombstones and seats the scope holds as it writes. The rule is the kernel's, in one call (`reservedRoleSetRefusedRole`, `reservedRoleSetRefusal`'s walk), with the caller's reservations joined with the scope class's. A seat made after the host's first check is seen. A CP-less restore (`restoreScopeLocal`, `restoreStagedScopeLocal`, given the vertical's roles) runs its role repair inside the load's own transaction, after the dump's rows are in: judged against exactly the seats the dump brings, and projected there, so a restore lands with its repair or not at all. A staged restore, whose first check sees the role set alone, is judged on its staged seats. The first checks stay, for their detailed wording. The in-unit refusal is one fixed sentence naming the role key only (`inUnitRoleRefusal`), and the unit writes nothing: a provision leaves no role row, tombstone, owner seat, receipt or enforcement flip, and a restore leaves the scope's rows and a staged load's stage as they were. **On a provision the scope's module migrations have already run when the in-unit check refuses**, as they do before every projection; only the projection is not written. The judged calls are RPC methods of their own (`applyJudgedProjectionReply`, `importDumpJudged`, `commitStagedDumpJudged`), so a host meeting a ScopeDO from before them is refused by the runtime with nothing written, rather than projecting unjudged. An older host keeps calling the methods it knows, which judge as before. A load that ran the repair answers `rolesProjected: true`. Without it (an older adapter, or a retry answered as already applied), `@substrat-run/vertical-host`'s restore routes repair with `projectRolesLocal` as before, which a current adapter also judges in its unit. #2555's second step can pass the directory's role set through the same load field.
- **Not checked yet.** A staff restore or import on a Cloudflare host the control plane runs reads the directory's roles in one call and loads the dump in the scope's next, so a role defined in that window is not judged against the dump (#2555). An import on such a host for a tenant with no live scope has no scope class to read, so it is judged with the host's own reservations alone: the seat rule holds without them, and a key only the class reserves is not counted. Also not checked: the directory record a shared control plane keeps of a connection grant whose serving deployment refuses it (it confers nothing, but stays listed until revoked), and grants and shape bindings recorded before these checks existed or before the key was reserved, with what reaches a person through them later, an older build's re-seat after a rollback included.
- **Decided, and left as is (#2531).** Each of these paths was weighed and is deliberately not judged by the rule:
  - **Data copies the platform makes:** a preview's in-deployment snapshot. It copies, at once, the role rows and seats a live scope holds, which were judged where they were written, and a copy revokes nothing, as no refusal does. A staff restore or import is no longer one of these (#2555, above).
  - **The directory fan-out** (`fanOut`, `projectScope`, and the ScopeDO's `applyProjection` they call): not judged again, and not flagged where it lands. The definition was judged where `defineRole` wrote it, and judging it again would drop a stored role and revoke its seats, or stop the tenant's scopes converging. The in-unit judgement above (#2531) is left off it for that reason: the fan-out calls the unjudged `applyProjectionReply`.
  - **The platform's role doors** (`HostAdmin.assignRole`, and `assignScopeRole` by default) keep the platform rule above. Telling a person from a host principal needs a record of what kind of principal each one is. That record belongs on the principal, written when the platform mints it, not on each seat, and it is decided with the host principal's lifecycle (not decided here, below) or made unnecessary by #1824 part 2, which removes the standing principal. Only staff and the platform's own service reach these doors today.
  - **A role defined on a Cloudflare coordinator for a tenant with no scope yet** is judged by the coordinator's modules alone, and the first scope's projection of it is not judged again. No product path reaches it: the dashboard, the one caller, defines its roles after the tenant's first scope exists, and in a package declaring a surface `lint:host-permissions` gives the host and its scope class the same `permissions`. Revisit when the dashboard, or any coordinator, registers a module that reserves a key.
  - **The owner grants an install gives:** an embedded install grants them on the app's scope, which judges them against its class's reservations, and a pushed manifest's are refused at push (`pushedOwnerGrantProblems`). A grant naming no scope, on a host that does not register the vertical's modules, is judged by that host's modules alone.
  - **A key reserved only through a schedule's `permissions`, on a host that registers no modules:** no product path defines a role on such a host, and push holds the registry's marks to the module declarations (#2466).
  - **The coordinator's scope-seat read before the ControlPlaneDO's `defineRole`:** the scopes and the directory are separate units and no fence spans them, so a scope-level seat made in between is not seen. The one caller is the dashboard, whose roles hold no reserved key. Revisit when the dashboard, or any coordinator, registers a module that reserves a key.
  - **The ScopeDO's one-row projection RPCs** (`projectRole`, `projectTenantTuple`, `revokeProjectedRole`) had no shipped caller and are removed, so every projected role row arrives through `applyProjection`.
  - **An adapter too old for the owner hand-over's person door:** the hand-over answers 501, asking for the adapter beside `@substrat-run/vertical-host` to be upgraded, when the scope host has no `scopeRoleRefusal`, rather than seating through the platform's door. A vertical pinning such a pair cannot hand its owner seat over until it upgrades.
- **What that door cannot tell.** A stored role now records its holder (#2444), but the door does not know whether the assignee is a person or a host principal. A correctly marked system-held role can still be assigned to a person at that door. One principal given two roles there can hold reserved keys and person keys together, which the engine test kit does, deliberately, to build a test principal. Telling a person from the host principal it is meant for needs a record of what kind of principal each one is, which nothing keeps yet.
- **Not decided here:** the host principal's lifecycle per team, and the actor and ceiling model that replaces it.

## Why

**The key is reserved because it writes the assistant's side of the record.** A person holding `assistant:run` could append events to their own thread and have the record say the assistant wrote them. The record is only evidence if that key is out of every person's reach.

**An exception that is declared is reviewable; one that is merely made is not.** The repo's checkpoint is a human reading a diff. A grant that never appears in the diff is outside it.
